# **me4us — Part 1: ME**

**You can't have US, if there's no ME.**

I think you've got the foundation of a genuinely good app concept here. The mistake most relationship apps make is treating the relationship as the starting point. **me4us starts with the individual.**

The airplane analogy is the perfect starting philosophy:

> You are not selfish for putting your oxygen mask on first. You're responsible for making sure you can breathe before trying to help everyone else.

Part 1 shouldn't just be a self-care checklist, a meditation app, or a mood tracker. It should help a person understand themselves, recognize where they're neglecting themselves, build healthy habits, and become a more emotionally, physically, mentally, and personally capable human being.

# **ME**

I take care of myself.

# **US**

We take care of each other.

# **US+**

We take care of our family.

The three sections should eventually feed into each other, but **ME should be a complete, valuable experience on its own**. It should not feel like a prerequisite or chore just to unlock the relationship portion.

# **2. What should the app ask?**

**The ME assessment**

I would not dump 100 questions on someone during signup. I'd make the first experience feel like a conversation with yourself. The app gradually learns about the person.

## **A. The initial ME check-in**

This is the baseline. It establishes how someone is doing right now.

The first 12 questions

| Question                                                              | What it measures             |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| 1. How are you feeling lately, honestly?                              | Emotional baseline           |
| 2. How much energy do you have on an average day?                     | Physical and mental energy   |
| 3. How well have you been sleeping?                                   | Sleep quality                |
| 4. How often do you make time for yourself?                           | Self-neglect / personal time |
| 5. How stressed do you feel lately?                                   | Stress burden                |
| 6. Are you doing things you genuinely enjoy?                          | Joy / recreation             |
| 7. How satisfied are you with the person you're becoming?             | Identity / growth            |
| 8. How do you speak to yourself when you make a mistake?              | Self-compassion / self-talk  |
| 9. Do you feel you can say no when you need to?                       | Boundaries                   |
| 10. Are there things you've been avoiding that weigh on you?          | Unresolved mental load       |
| 11. When was the last time you did something just for yourself?       | Personal autonomy            |
| 12. What is one thing you wish were different in your life right now? | Personal priority            |

I'd use a mixture of scales, choices and free-text answers.

For example:

The key is not making people feel diagnosed, judged or graded.

## **B. Questions organized by the six pillars**

The app should have a question bank. Not every question is asked every day. Some are initial assessment questions, some weekly reflections, some triggered by patterns, and others are optional deep dives.

### **1. My body**

Objective: identify physical habits that affect quality of life.

**Questions:**

- How many hours did you sleep last night?
- How rested do you feel when you wake up?
- How often are you physically active?
- Have you been drinking enough water?
- How would you describe your eating habits lately?
- How much time have you spent outside this week?
- Are you experiencing physical discomfort you have been ignoring?
- Have you scheduled the health appointments you need?
- Do you have a consistent bedtime?
- How often do you feel physically exhausted?

**What the app does:**

- Sleep tracking.
- Water tracking.
- Movement tracking.
- Exercise goals.
- Personal health appointment reminders.
- Sleep routines.
- Rest days.
- Simple habit building.

Important: The app should not make weight, calorie consumption, body size or fitness performance the primary measure of health. These can be optional, but the core should be how a person feels and functions.

### **2. My mind & emotions**

Objective: help people understand their emotional states rather than suppress them.

**Questions:**

- What emotions have you experienced most this week?
- What has been bothering you?
- What is consuming the most mental energy?
- What makes you feel calm?
- How do you usually respond when you're angry?
- How do you react when you feel overwhelmed?
- Do you feel you have someone you can talk to?
- What are you worrying about that you cannot control?
- Have you been mentally exhausted?
- What do you need right now that you haven't been giving yourself?

**What the app does:**

- Daily mood logging.
- Emotion identification.
- Stress tracking.
- Trigger identification.
- Guided breathing.
- Journaling.
- Thought reframing exercises.
- Guided decompression.
- Reflection prompts.

A feature I would absolutely build: What's underneath?

Someone logs:

I'M ANGRY

Instead of stopping at anger, the app gently explores:

- Am I actually angry, or hurt?
- Do I feel ignored?
- Am I exhausted?
- Am I feeling unfairly treated?
- Is something outside my control making me feel powerless?
- Do I need to act, or do I need to let this go?

The objective isn't to tell someone what they're feeling. It's to help them identify what they might be feeling.

### **3. Who am I?**

Objective: prevent someone from losing their identity in the roles they occupy.

People become spouses. Parents. Employees. Business owners. Caregivers. Providers.

Sometimes they forget that they were people before those roles.

**Questions:**

- What do you enjoy doing?
- What are you curious about?
- What hobby have you abandoned?
- What makes you feel alive?
- What did you enjoy before life became so busy?
- What would you do if you had three free hours?
- What do you wish people knew about you?
- Are you spending time with people who make you feel good?
- Do you feel like you're living your life, or just fulfilling obligations?
- What part of yourself have you neglected?

**What the app does:**

- Personal interest inventory.
- Hobby rediscovery.
- Personal time scheduling.
- Bucket list.
- Personal experiences list.
- Social connection goals.
- Individual activity recommendations.

Feature idea: The forgotten ME

A periodic experience where the app reminds someone of things they used to love, or helps them discover something new.

For example:

> You mentioned three months ago that you wanted to learn to play guitar.

> You've had 14 days of work, obligations and routines. How about 30 minutes for something that belongs only to you?

### **5. Self-respect & boundaries**

Objective: build the ability to respect oneself without becoming self-centered.

This pillar is particularly important because it feeds directly into Part 2, US.

**Questions:**

- Do you say yes when you really want to say no?
- Do you feel guilty when you take time for yourself?
- Are you allowing people to treat you in ways you don't accept?
- Do you frequently apologize when you haven't done anything wrong?
- Are you holding yourself to standards you would never impose on others?
- Do you keep promises you make to yourself?
- Are you avoiding a conversation you need to have?
- Do you recognize when you are wrong?
- How do you respond to criticism?
- Are you treating yourself with the same respect you expect from others?

**What the app does:**

- Boundary-setting exercises.
- Personal values.
- Self-talk exercises.
- Personal commitments.
- Accountability.
- Conflict reflection.
- Assertiveness practice.

Feature idea: Keep a promise to ME

Users make small promises to themselves.

Examples:

- I will take a 20-minute walk today.
- I will not work past 8 PM tonight.
- I will call my friend.
- I will take a break without guilt.
- I will finish the task I keep avoiding.

The app tracks promises kept, not perfection.

Breaking a commitment should prompt an adjustment, not punishment. Otherwise, the app becomes another source of guilt.

# **3. What should ME track?**

The trick is not tracking everything. If you track 47 things, you don't build self-care; you build a data-entry job.

I'd divide tracking into three levels.

## **Level 1 — Daily signals**

**The daily 60-second check-in**

# **Today, how am I?**

Six quick signals. No pressure. No perfection.

My mood

1–5

My energy

1–5

My stress

1–5

My sleep

Hours / quality

Did I do something for ME?

Yes / No

What do I need today?

Quick selection

The last question could have selectable options:

- Rest
- Movement
- Connection
- Quiet
- Fun
- Focus
- Help
- Space
- Purpose

The app should adapt based on that answer.

If someone selects **Rest**, perhaps it suggests a wind-down routine.

If someone selects **Connection**, perhaps it suggests calling a friend.

If someone selects **Focus**, perhaps it suggests a 10-minute task.

This makes the app responsive rather than merely recording data.

## **Level 2 — Habit and behavior tracking**

Users should be able to select their own habits, with suggested habits organized by pillar.

| Pillar     | Suggested habits                                           |
| ---------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Body       | Walk, stretch, sleep on time, hydrate, exercise            |
| Mind       | Journal, meditate, breathe, take a quiet break             |
| Identity   | Read for pleasure, hobby time, creative activity           |
| Growth     | Study, practice a skill, work on a personal goal           |
| Boundaries | Say no, take a break, keep a personal promise              |
| Life       | Organize, manage finances, connect with a friend, plan fun |

Each habit should support:

- Daily, weekly or custom schedules.
- Completion tracking.
- Flexible targets.
- Streaks, but without penalizing missed days.
- Habit history.
- Personal notes.
- Optional reminders.
- The ability to pause or delete habits.

## **Level 3 — Weekly and monthly insight**

This is where the app becomes substantially more useful than a checklist.

### **Weekly reflection**

Once a week, the app should generate a short, meaningful reflection.

YOUR WEEK WITH ME

# **Your weekly reflection**

Illustrative example of what the app might generate.

Your energy

You reported low energy on four of the seven days.

Your mood

Your mood improved on days when you recorded personal activities.

Your commitments

You completed 4 of your 5 personal habits.

Something worth noticing

You consistently reported stress on days when you slept fewer than six hours.

Your next small step

Try protecting 30 minutes of personal downtime three evenings this week.

The system should distinguish correlation from causation. It should say *you noticed*, *this appears related*, or *a pattern may be emerging*, rather than pretending it can diagnose someone from seven check-ins.

### **Monthly review**

Once a month, the user should see:

- Mood trends.
- Energy trends.
- Sleep trends.
- Stress trends.
- Habit consistency.
- Personal goal progress.
- Time spent on personal activities.
- Promises kept.
- Biggest stressors.
- Biggest sources of happiness.
- Changes in personal priorities.

And one important question:

> Are you becoming the person you want to be?

That question should periodically appear throughout the app.

## **D. ME Journal**

Not a generic notebook. A structured personal journal with optional AI assistance.

### **Journal modes**

- Free writing — Write anything.
- Daily reflection — What happened today?
- Emotional processing — What am I feeling, and why?
- Gratitude — What was good today?
- Problem-solving — What is the problem? What can I control?
- Personal goals — What progress did I make?
- Self-reflection — What did I learn about myself?
- Brain dump — Empty my head.

### **AI journal assistant**

The AI could help the user explore what they write, but should never be judgmental or pretend to be a therapist.

Example:

> You mentioned feeling frustrated three times this week. Would you like to explore what those situations had in common?

Or:

> You seem to be putting a lot of pressure on yourself to get everything done. What would happen if one of those tasks waited until tomorrow?

The user should explicitly control whether the AI can analyze their journal. Journal privacy should be a core product feature, not a footnote.

# **5. What makes ME connect to US and US+?**

This is the most important architectural decision.

**ME is private by default.**

Part 1 should be the individual's personal space.

The spouse should not automatically see:

- Mood entries.
- Journals.
- Stress levels.
- Personal goals.
- Individual habit history.
- Personal reflections.
- Private answers.

However, the user can voluntarily share certain outcomes.

For example:

| Private ME information     | Optional shared outcome                          |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| I am feeling overwhelmed.  | I need a little more quiet time.                 |
| My energy has been low.    | I would like to have a more restful weekend.     |
| I am working on patience.  | I'm trying to improve how I react when stressed. |
| I need more personal time. | I'd like us to make room for individual time.    |
| I want to exercise.        | I'd like us to go for walks together.            |
| I need social connection.  | I'd like to plan a night with friends.           |

The app should never turn the individual's private self-care into a surveillance tool for their spouse.

This creates a natural bridge:

- **ME:** I discover what I need.
- **US:** I communicate what I need and what I can contribute.
- **US+:** We apply those skills to our family.

# **6. The ME scoring system — should there be one?**

**My opinion: Yes to insights. No to a score that judges a person.**

I would not create a score of 78/100 that tells someone they're a bad human because they didn't meditate, drink eight glasses of water and read a book this week.

Instead, create a **ME Balance** visualization.

Perhaps a user is thriving in career and fitness but struggling with stress and personal identity. The app helps them see the imbalance, rather than handing them a generic prescription.

# **7. The first 30 days of ME**

I wouldn't throw the whole app at a new user. I'd introduce the system progressively.

| Week   | Theme       | Objective                                         |
| ------ | ----------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Week 1 | Discover ME | Understand your baseline and needs                |
| Week 2 | Care for ME | Build small daily care habits                     |
| Week 3 | Respect ME  | Boundaries, self-talk, promises and personal time |
| Week 4 | Grow ME     | Goals, purpose, hobbies and progress              |

### **Week 1: Discover**

- Initial assessment.
- Daily check-ins.
- Mood identification.
- Stress identification.
- Sleep and energy awareness.
- Identify neglected areas.

### **Week 2: Care**

- Select two or three personal habits.
- Create a simple routine.
- Try daily ME activities.
- Track personal time.
- Review energy and mood.

### **Week 3: Respect**

- Identify boundaries.
- Practice saying no.
- Make one promise to yourself.
- Explore self-talk.
- Identify one recurring problem.

### **Week 4: Grow**

- Set one personal goal.
- Choose a hobby or interest.
- Set milestones.
- Review your month.
- Build your personal ME plan.

These are not mandatory levels or achievements. The user can move freely through the app. The 30-day journey is a guided onboarding experience, not a gate.

# **9. The philosophy of me4us**

I'd make the core message something people genuinely believe, not just marketing.

# **me4us**

# ***You can't have US, if there's no ME.***

You spend your life taking care of things.

Your work. Your responsibilities. The people you love. The things that need fixing. The things that need doing.

Somewhere along the way, you can forget to take care of the person doing all of it.

That person is you.

ME isn't about being selfish. It's about recognizing that your well-being matters, too.

Because being a better partner, a better parent, and a better person doesn't begin with doing more for everyone else.

It begins with taking care of yourself.

My opinion: This should be the emotional backbone of the entire app. Not productivity. Not gamification. Not relationship hacks. Personal well-being, self-awareness and intentional growth, which then become the foundation for healthier relationships and families.

Confidence in this product direction: High.

The next step I would take is to design the actual **ME module in detail** — the screens, navigation, complete questionnaire, question logic, habit system, daily check-in, activities, data model and what the app does with the answers. That is where we turn this philosophy into an actual product.
