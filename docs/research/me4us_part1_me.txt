me4us — PART 1: ME
You can't have US, if there's no ME.

FOUNDATION
The foundation of me4us is the individual. The app begins with self-care, self-awareness and personal growth: being a better person to yourself so you can be a better partner and parent.

The airplane analogy captures the philosophy: put your oxygen mask on first, then help others. Taking care of yourself is not selfish; it is part of being able to show up for others.

Part 1 should be a complete experience on its own, not a chore or prerequisite for unlocking the relationship portions.

1. WHAT ME ACCOMPLISHES: SIX PILLARS

1) PHYSICAL WELL-BEING — MY BODY
Sleep, energy, movement, nutrition, hydration, physical health, rest and habits.

2) MENTAL & EMOTIONAL WELL-BEING — MY MIND
Stress, emotions, anxiety, frustration, self-awareness, coping mechanisms, emotional regulation and mental load.

3) PERSONAL IDENTITY — WHO I AM
Values, interests, passions, individuality, personal goals, hobbies, confidence and identity beyond roles.

4) GROWTH & PURPOSE — WHO I WANT TO BECOME
Learning, ambition, personal goals, skills, discipline, achievements and purpose.

5) SELF-RESPECT & BOUNDARIES — HOW I TREAT MYSELF
Boundaries, self-talk, self-worth, saying no, recognizing needs, personal responsibility and accountability.

6) LIFE & SOCIAL WELL-BEING — MY LIFE OUTSIDE MY ROLES
Friendships, finances, environment, fun, social connections, life satisfaction and everyday responsibilities.

These are lenses for examining life, not six more obligations.

2. WHAT ME SHOULD ASK

INITIAL ASSESSMENT: 12 QUESTIONS
1. How are you feeling lately, honestly?
2. How much energy do you have on an average day?
3. How well have you been sleeping?
4. How often do you make time for yourself?
5. How stressed do you feel lately?
6. Are you doing things you genuinely enjoy?
7. How satisfied are you with the person you're becoming?
8. How do you speak to yourself when you make a mistake?
9. Do you feel you can say no when you need to?
10. Are there things you've been avoiding that weigh on you?
11. When was the last time you did something just for yourself?
12. What is one thing you wish were different in your life right now?

Use a mixture of scales, choices and optional free text. Avoid making users feel diagnosed, judged or graded.

PILLAR QUESTION BANKS

MY BODY
- How many hours did you sleep last night?
- How rested do you feel when you wake up?
- How often are you physically active?
- Have you been drinking enough water?
- How would you describe your eating habits lately?
- How much time have you spent outside this week?
- Are you experiencing physical discomfort you have been ignoring?
- Have you scheduled the health appointments you need?
- Do you have a consistent bedtime?
- How often do you feel physically exhausted?

Functions: sleep, water, movement and exercise tracking; routines; rest days; personal health reminders; habit building. Weight, calories and body size should not be central measures.

MY MIND & EMOTIONS
- What emotions have you experienced most this week?
- What has been bothering you?
- What is consuming the most mental energy?
- What makes you feel calm?
- How do you usually respond when you're angry?
- How do you react when you feel overwhelmed?
- Do you feel you have someone you can talk to?
- What are you worrying about that you cannot control?
- Have you been mentally exhausted?
- What do you need right now that you haven't been giving yourself?

Functions: mood logging, emotion identification, stress tracking, trigger identification, guided breathing, journaling, thought reframing, decompression and reflection.

FEATURE: WHAT'S UNDERNEATH?
Help users explore anger or frustration:
- Am I angry, or hurt?
- Do I feel ignored?
- Am I exhausted?
- Am I feeling unfairly treated?
- Is something outside my control making me feel powerless?
- Do I need to act, or do I need to let this go?

The app should help users explore, not dictate their feelings.

WHO AM I?
- What do you enjoy doing?
- What are you curious about?
- What hobby have you abandoned?
- What makes you feel alive?
- What did you enjoy before life became so busy?
- What would you do if you had three free hours?
- What do you wish people knew about you?
- Are you spending time with people who make you feel good?
- Do you feel like you're living your life, or just fulfilling obligations?
- What part of yourself have you neglected?

Functions: personal interest inventory, hobby rediscovery, personal time scheduling, bucket list, experiences, social connection goals and activity suggestions.

FEATURE: THE FORGOTTEN ME
Periodically resurface interests or goals the user mentioned but neglected.
Example: “You mentioned three months ago that you wanted to learn guitar. How about 30 minutes for something that belongs only to you?”

GROWTH & PURPOSE
- What do you want to accomplish this year?
- What skill do you want to learn?
- What is something you've been putting off?
- What would make you proud six months from now?
- What are you currently working toward?
- Are you making progress?
- What is holding you back?
- Is your goal truly yours, or something you feel pressured to do?
- What would the next smallest step be?
- What does success mean to you today?

Functions: goals, milestones, small steps, skill development, personal challenges, weekly reviews and reassessment. Goals could be learning, business, reading, patience, debt reduction, travel, fitness or confidence.

SELF-RESPECT & BOUNDARIES
- Do you say yes when you really want to say no?
- Do you feel guilty when you take time for yourself?
- Are you allowing people to treat you in ways you don't accept?
- Do you frequently apologize when you haven't done anything wrong?
- Are you holding yourself to standards you would never impose on others?
- Do you keep promises you make to yourself?
- Are you avoiding a conversation you need to have?
- Do you recognize when you are wrong?
- How do you respond to criticism?
- Are you treating yourself with the same respect you expect from others?

Functions: boundary-setting, personal values, self-talk, personal commitments, accountability, conflict reflection and assertiveness practice.

FEATURE: KEEP A PROMISE TO ME
Users make small promises, such as a walk, taking a break, calling a friend or finishing an avoided task. Track promises kept, not perfection. A missed commitment should prompt adjustment, not punishment.

MY LIFE
- How satisfied are you with your financial situation?
- Do you know where your money goes?
- Is your living environment helping or hurting your mental well-being?
- Do you feel overwhelmed by your responsibilities?
- Are you maintaining friendships?
- Do you have a social life outside your immediate family?
- Are you having enough fun?
- How much of your day is spent doing things you don't want to do?
- Is your life reasonably organized?
- What is the biggest source of chaos in your life right now?

Functions: personal finance habits, life organization, decluttering challenges, social connection reminders, calendar-based personal time, personal task lists, fun and leisure planning.

3. WHAT ME SHOULD TRACK

LEVEL 1 — DAILY SIGNALS: 60-SECOND CHECK-IN
- Mood (1–5)
- Energy (1–5)
- Stress (1–5)
- Sleep (hours and/or quality)
- Did I do something for ME? (yes/no)
- What do I need today? (quick selection)

Possible needs: Rest, Movement, Connection, Quiet, Fun, Focus, Help, Space, Purpose.

The app can adapt: Rest suggests wind-down; Connection suggests reaching out to a friend; Focus suggests a small task. This is responsive rather than merely recording data.

LEVEL 2 — HABITS
Suggested habits:
- Body: walk, stretch, sleep on time, hydrate, exercise.
- Mind: journal, meditate, breathe, take a quiet break.
- Identity: read for pleasure, hobby time, creative activity.
- Growth: study, practice a skill, work on a personal goal.
- Boundaries: say no, take a break, keep a personal promise.
- Life: organize, manage finances, connect with a friend, plan fun.

Habits support daily/weekly/custom schedules, completion tracking, flexible targets, non-punitive streaks, history, notes, optional reminders and pause/delete.

LEVEL 3 — WEEKLY & MONTHLY INSIGHTS

Weekly review:
- Energy and mood patterns
- Stress patterns
- Habit consistency
- Personal goal progress
- Personal activities
- Promises kept
- Biggest stressors and sources of happiness
- A next small step

Be careful about causal claims. Use “you noticed,” “this appears related,” or “a pattern may be emerging,” not diagnoses.

Monthly review:
- Mood, energy, sleep and stress trends
- Habit consistency
- Personal goal progress
- Time spent on personal activities
- Promises kept
- Biggest stressors
- Biggest sources of happiness
- Changes in priorities

Periodically ask: ARE YOU BECOMING THE PERSON YOU WANT TO BE?

4. WHAT THE APP SHOULD DO

A) ME DASHBOARD
- How am I? — daily check-in
- Today's ME — personalized daily activity
- My habits — daily progress
- My goals — personal progress
- My journal — private reflection
- My trends — mood, energy and behavioral patterns
- My challenges — optional short challenges
- My toolbox — exercises, activities and resources

Keep the dashboard uncluttered and immediately actionable.

B) TODAY'S ME — PERSONALIZED ACTIVITIES
Examples:
- Move: take a 15-minute walk, ideally without your phone.
- Reflect: write what's weighing on you (5 minutes).
- Reconnect with yourself: do something enjoyable for 20 minutes.
- Reset: breathe and decompress for five minutes.

Recommendation logic prioritizes:
1. Current emotional and physical state
2. Needs identified by the user
3. Habits they are building
4. Goals established
5. Things they've been neglecting
6. Variety and avoiding repetition
7. Available time

Users can say “I have 5 minutes” or “I have an hour” and get an appropriately sized activity. Avoid overwhelming people who are already struggling.

C) ME TOOLBOX
Library categories:
- Emotional reset: breathing, grounding, calming and processing emotions.
- Self-reflection: prompts, gratitude, thought exploration and patterns.
- Movement: short workouts, stretching, walking and mobility.
- Learning: skill-building, mini lessons and learning challenges.
- Personal growth: goal planning and breaking tasks into steps.
- Joy: fun, hobbies, experiences, social connection and play.
- Rest: sleep routines, relaxation, recovery and downtime.
- Confidence & boundaries: assertiveness, values, self-respect and communication practice.

Each activity should include duration, difficulty, purpose, instructions, optional guided interaction and reflection.

D) ME JOURNAL
Modes:
- Free writing
- Daily reflection
- Emotional processing
- Gratitude
- Problem-solving
- Personal goals
- Self-reflection
- Brain dump

An optional AI journal assistant can help explore themes, but it should not judge or pretend to be a therapist. Examples:
- “You mentioned feeling frustrated three times this week. Would you like to explore what those situations had in common?”
- “You seem to be putting a lot of pressure on yourself to get everything done. What would happen if one of those tasks waited until tomorrow?”

The user controls whether AI can analyze their journal. Privacy is a core feature.

5. HOW ME CONNECTS TO US AND US+

ME IS PRIVATE BY DEFAULT.
Spouses should not automatically see mood entries, journals, stress levels, personal goals, habit history or private reflections. Users may voluntarily share outcomes:
- “I need a little more quiet time.”
- “I'd like a more restful weekend.”
- “I'm trying to improve how I react when stressed.”
- “I'd like us to make room for individual time.”
- “I'd like us to go for walks together.”
- “I'd like to plan a night with friends.”

The bridge:
- ME: I discover what I need.
- US: I communicate what I need and what I can contribute.
- US+: We apply those skills to our family.

Never turn private self-care into a spouse surveillance tool.

6. ME BALANCE: INSIGHTS, NOT JUDGMENT

Avoid a simplistic score that judges a person. Instead, use a ME Balance visualization across the six pillars, reflecting perceived satisfaction and personal priorities.

Help users identify:
- Where they're thriving
- Where they're struggling
- What they want to prioritize
- How things have changed over time

The user determines what matters most. This is not a clinical assessment or objective measurement of health.

7. FIRST 30 DAYS

Week 1 — Discover ME
Objective: Understand baseline and needs.
- Initial assessment
- Daily check-ins
- Emotion and stress identification
- Sleep and energy awareness
- Identify neglected areas

Week 2 — Care for ME
Objective: Build small daily care habits.
- Select 2–3 habits
- Create a simple routine
- Try daily activities
- Track personal time
- Review energy and mood

Week 3 — Respect ME
Objective: Boundaries, self-talk, promises and personal time.
- Identify boundaries
- Practice saying no
- Make a promise to yourself
- Explore self-talk
- Identify a recurring problem

Week 4 — Grow ME
Objective: Goals, purpose, hobbies and progress.
- Set a personal goal
- Choose a hobby
- Set milestones
- Review the month
- Build a personal ME plan

This is guided onboarding, not a gate or mandatory progression.

8. MVP: BUILD THESE EIGHT COMPONENTS FIRST
1. ME Assessment: baseline questionnaire with six pillars.
2. Daily Check-In: mood, energy, stress, sleep and needs.
3. Habits: flexible habits, history and reminders.
4. Today's ME: personalized activities.
5. Journal: private reflection, prompts and free writing.
6. Personal Goals: goals, milestones and progress.
7. ME Balance & Insights: trends, reflections and patterns.
8. ME Toolbox: exercises, activities and guided challenges.

9. THE PHILOSOPHY OF me4us

me4us
You can't have US, if there's no ME.

You spend your life taking care of things.

Your work. Your responsibilities. The people you love. The things that need fixing. The things that need doing.

Somewhere along the way, you can forget to take care of the person doing all of it.

That person is you.

ME isn't about being selfish. It's about recognizing that your well-being matters, too.

Because being a better partner, a better parent, and a better person doesn't begin with doing more for everyone else.

IT BEGINS WITH TAKING CARE OF YOURSELF.

PRODUCT DIRECTION
Personal well-being, self-awareness and intentional growth are the foundation for healthier relationships and families. Not productivity for its own sake, not guilt, and not superficial gamification.
